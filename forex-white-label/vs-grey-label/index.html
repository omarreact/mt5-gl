White Label vs Grey Label: Which Model Fits Your Brokerage?

Ask ten people in this industry to explain the difference and you’ll probably get ten slightly different answers. Some providers use the terms loosely.…

Talk to MetaGreyLabel ↗Explore Grey Label

Ask ten people in this industry to explain the difference and you’ll probably get ten slightly different answers. Some providers use the terms loosely. Others blur them on purpose to make a cheaper package sound like more than it is.

That’s a problem when you’re about to commit real money. This page breaks down white label vs grey label in plain language, so you can see what each model gives you, what it costs you in control, and which one makes more sense for where your brokerage is right now.

A white label lets you trade under your own brand on someone else’s MetaTrader server, with limited control over how that server is run.

A grey label also runs on an existing server, but you get your own manager access and much more say over trading conditions, groups and risk.

Both are far cheaper than buying a full MetaTrader licence. The real question is how much independence you need today.

01

The Difference Between White Label and Grey Label Forex

To understand the difference between white label and grey label forex setups properly, it helps to look at a few specific areas.

Control Over Trading Conditions

On a white label, many of your settings sit under the main server’s oversight. You may be able to set some spreads and commissions, but leverage limits, symbol settings and group structures are often restricted.

On a grey label, those decisions are largely yours. You can build your own groups, set your own spreads and markups, adjust leverage and decide which instruments each client type can trade.

Manager and Admin Access

White label brokers usually get a limited back office view. Enough to see clients and accounts, but not much more.

Grey label brokers get proper manager terminal access. Your dealing team can monitor exposure, adjust positions, manage groups and react to market conditions without waiting on anyone else.

Risk Management

This is one of the biggest practical differences. With a white label, risk is often handled at the main server level, which limits how you can run your book.

With a grey label, you have far more flexibility. You can decide how much flow to route to liquidity providers, how much to keep internally and how to treat different client groups. For brokers who want to grow revenue seriously, that matters a lot.

Branding

Both models let you put your name on the platform, the client portal and your website. Traders won’t see the provider behind you in either case. Branding isn’t really where they differ.

Cost

A white label is the cheaper option, both in setup fee and monthly fee. A grey label costs more because you’re getting more control and more server resources. Even so, both sit well below the cost of a full licence and your own infrastructure.

Speed to Launch

Both can go live quickly once your paperwork is ready. A white label is sometimes a little faster simply because there’s less to configure.

02

When a White Label Makes Sense

A white label is a smart choice when you want to get started with minimal risk and keep early costs low.

It fits introducing brokers taking their first step towards their own brand, founders testing whether a particular market will respond, and businesses adding forex as a side offering rather than a core product. If you don’t yet have a dealing team or a clear risk strategy, a white label also takes a lot of that pressure off.

The trade-off is control. Once your client numbers grow, the restrictions usually start to feel tight.

03

When a Grey Label Makes Sense

A grey label suits brokers who already know their market and want room to run things their own way.

It works well if you have steady client volumes, a team that understands dealing and risk, or plans to fine-tune spreads and conditions for different client types. It’s also the natural step for white label brokers who’ve outgrown their setup but aren’t ready for the cost of a full licence.

You get much of the independence of owning a server, without the heavy infrastructure bill.

04

White Label vs Grey Label: Which Is Better for Brokers?

There’s no universal answer to which is better for brokers, and anyone who tells you otherwise is probably trying to sell you one in particular. It really depends on your stage, your team and your budget.

Ask Yourself a Few Questions

How many active clients do you expect in your first year? Do you have someone who can manage a dealing desk? Do you need custom groups and trading conditions from day one, or can that wait? How much are you comfortable spending upfront before you’ve proven the model?

If most of your answers lean towards “not yet,” a white label is probably the right start. If you’ve already got clients, a team and a clear plan, a grey label will likely serve you better.

You Don’t Have to Decide Forever

This is the part people often miss. Starting on one model doesn’t lock you in. Plenty of our clients launch as a white label, prove their market, then move to a grey label within a year or two. Because both run on the same infrastructure with us, the switch is smooth and your clients barely notice.

05

06

Still Weighing White Label vs Grey Label?

The white label vs grey label decision is one of the most important you’ll make early on, and it’s worth getting a second opinion before you commit.

FAQs:H3: Can my clients tell whether I’m on a white label or a grey label?

No. In both cases, traders see your brand on the platform, the client portal and your website. The difference is in how much control you have behind the scenes, not in what your clients see when they log in.

Is a grey label much more expensive than a white label?

It costs more, both in setup and monthly fees, because you’re getting manager access, more server resources and greater control over trading conditions. That said, both models are still far cheaper than buying a full MetaTrader licence and running your own infrastructure.

How long does it take to move from a white label to a grey label?

Usually not long, as both run on the same infrastructure with us. We plan the switch around your trading hours, move your groups and client accounts across, and test everything before it goes live. Most clients barely notice the change.

Do I need a licence for either model?

That depends on where you’re based and which markets you want to serve. Some jurisdictions require a licence before you accept clients, whichever model you choose. We can share what we’ve seen work for other brokers, but your legal advisor should confirm what applies to you.
